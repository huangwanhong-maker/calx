# Release Notes: 23 October 2026

## 1. Fourier Transforms and Spectra

In this update calx can take the fast Fourier transform of a sequence of any length, prime lengths included, and estimate a signal's power spectrum by Welch's method. They agree with the defining sums to fifteen digits. The neuroscience domain uses them to find the rhythms of spiking networks.

## 2. The Compiler's First Targets

In this update calx's plan names the first programs its compute stack must compile: the neuroscience domain's spiking networks and quantum trajectories ([idea](../ideas/2026-10-23-spiking-and-quantum-targets.md)). Its intermediate representation will carry events, sparse delivery at delays and stateful synapses from the start, and its backends will reach neuromorphic chips and quantum processors. Nothing of this is built yet; today's networks are simulated on the CPU.

## 3. The First Numerical Modules

In this update calx has its first code: the numerical methods the neuroscience domain's spiking networks need ([decision 0004](../decisions/0004-first-modules-and-the-build.md)). It now offers:

- random numbers that come out the same whatever the number of threads;
- the exponential of a matrix, small dense solves and sparse matrices by rows;
- Runge–Kutta integrators, including an adaptive one;
- the scaled error function, adaptive integration and root finding;
- a pool of threads for parallel phases.

Each module is checked against published reference values or closed forms, and every check reports in the same way as the rest of the project's checks. The library builds with CMake, and the rest of its plan is set out in the project's [map of the mathematics](https://github.com/huangwanhong-maker/The-Science-Infrastructures/blob/main/docs/mathematics.md).
