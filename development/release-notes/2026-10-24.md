# Release Notes: 24 October 2026

## 1. Conjugate Gradients

In this update calx solves large sparse symmetric systems by conjugate gradients, preconditioned by their diagonal ([decision 0007](../decisions/0007-conjugate-gradients.md)). Singular ones, such as the Laplacians of a graph or a complex, are solved too, to their solution of least norm when their kernel is given. The infrastructure's Hodge decompositions of thousands of cells, the circulation of value between economies and across months, run on it.

## 2. Vector Lanes, Task Graphs and What the Genome Asks of calx

In this update calx can run one kernel on the widest vectors the processor has, written once: `Lanes<T, W>` compiles to SSE, AVX2, AVX-512 or NEON, with saturating arithmetic where the instructions exist and the same results where they do not, and `calx::cpu` tells which processor it runs on and what the build was allowed. Work that is not uniform runs as a task graph, its ready tasks taken by their upward rank as HEFT takes them, with a trace of which thread ran what and when, or as a dynamic loop of chunks; both give the same answers on any number of threads. The numerical methods gain the symmetric eigenproblem (Householder and the implicit QL algorithm), LU factors and determinants with their logarithm, the gamma family (log Γ, ψ, ψ′, the incomplete gamma and beta functions and the χ², binomial, Student and F tails), gamma, beta and binomial variates, and Brent's minimiser of a function of one variable, the first content of `optimisation`. The genomics and evolution domains of The Science Infrastructures align sequences on these lanes, count k-mers and bootstrap trees on these graphs, and fit their models with these functions ([decision 0006](../decisions/0006-lanes-task-graphs-and-the-genome.md)).
