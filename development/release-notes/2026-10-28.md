# Release Notes: 28 October 2026

## 1. Real-Time Scheduling; Schmidt's Associated Legendre Functions

In this update calx schedules work that has deadlines, and evaluates the Legendre functions of spherical-harmonic fields ([decision 0009](../decisions/0009-real-time-scheduling.md)).

- **Tasks with deadlines.** A periodic task is a cost, a period, a deadline, a phase, a priority and a criticality. calx says beforehand whether a set can be met: by its utilisation and Liu and Layland's bound, by the exact response times of fixed priorities with blocking, and by EDF's processor-demand criterion for deadlines shorter than periods.
- **Shares and starvation.** A constant bandwidth server gives a stream of jobs of unknown cost, such as a storm of events, a budget every period and no more, so that it cannot starve the others. Aging under fixed priorities bounds how long a low job waits.
- **Overload.** Elastic compression stretches the periods of the more elastic tasks until the set fits a desired utilisation, leaving rigid ones as they are.
- **Simulation and dispatch.** A deterministic simulation runs any set on several processors, preemptive or not, by EDF, fixed priority with or without aging, or FIFO, with servers, and returns every slice and every job's lateness. A dispatcher runs released jobs on the pool of threads in the policy's order and measures their lateness on the host's clock. Cosmopoiesis schedules its instruments with it.
- **Legendre functions.** The associated Legendre functions in Schmidt's semi-normalisation, the functions of geomagnetism's models, come with their derivatives and with their quotient by the cosine of latitude, finite at the poles. They moved here from the geophysics domain, which reads the World Magnetic Model with them, and are held to the standard library's own implementation.

The checks hold the analyses to the simulation on hundreds of random task sets, the server's rules to cases worked by hand, aging to the instant a starvation ends, and the Legendre functions to their normalisation, closed forms, derivatives and polar limits.
